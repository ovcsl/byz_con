.180Condictio ex mutuo0Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive passage is the last explicit condictio: someone gives another’s money causa credendi, then becomes furiosus, and the money is consumed. The claim is for a personal condictio arising from the credit/loan-like transaction and consumption, not for vindication of a subsisting specific thing owned by the claimant. Because the money has been consumed and is not reclaimed in specie as property, the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: a person gives another’s money credendi causa, later becomes insane, and the money is consumed. The phrase credendi causa presents the giving as a credit/mutuum, and the use of another’s coins affects ownership transfer, not the parties’ loan intention. Thus the passage shows consensus directed at a loan for consumption.
.1.2.270Datio (transfer of ownership)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: a person gives another’s money credendi causa, later becomes insane, and the money is consumed. Because the money is expressly aliena pecunia, the giver is not shown to have authority to alienate it, so ownership could not pass by a valid mutuum datio. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
