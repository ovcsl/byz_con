.6.3.116Pecunia soluta (the money has been paid)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: fruits taken by a tenant after the completed five-year term, recoverable only if not taken with the owner’s consent. Although the opening sentence mentions a pledged thing after payment of the money, that is an earlier distinct scenario and is not decisive here. The last scenario does not show discharge of a secured debt or retention of a pledge after payment. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
.6.3.217Ex iniusta causa (under unlawful cause)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: a colonus takes fruits after the completed lustrum. The text allows condictio only if the fruits were not taken by the owner's will, and expressly says that with the owner's consent the condictio ceases. Thus the asserted condictio rests on possession without a valid causa. Conclusion: satisfied.
.6.3.319Post lustrum completum (after his term has ended)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: a colonus takes fruits after the five-year term has been completed. The text says the fruits are condicible only if they were not taken by the owner’s will; if taken with consent, the condictio ceases. Thus it shows post-term taking without consent, where the former relationship no longer supplies a legal ground. Conclusion: satisfied.
.6.3.426Vi fluminum importata (swept in by force of rivers)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: a colonus who, after the five-year term is complete, takes fruits, which are condicted only if not taken by the owner’s will. The fruits do not arrive through natural forces as ownerless things, nor is there a new natural acquisition of ownership. The passage turns on lack or presence of the dominus’ voluntas, so the criterion is not satisfied.
.6.3.558Ex causa furtiva (by way of theft)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: the colonus who, after the five-year term is complete, takes fruits. The text allows condictio only if the fruits were taken not by the owner’s will, and says it ceases if they were taken with consent. Thus the decisive passage shows a taking against the owner’s will and no lawful ground for retention. Conclusion: satisfied.
