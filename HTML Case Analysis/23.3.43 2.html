.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the extraneus who arranged the acceptilatio so that, upon dissolution of the marriage, he has a condictio. This concerns restoration or recovery connected with a dowry obligation and its discharge, not reclaiming a specific thing as continuing owner. The claim is personal and obligation-based, not vindication-like. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

No mutual intention to create a mutuum appears. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.3.291Person Z is enriched at the expense of person Y, who should have had property from X. 0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the clause where the extraneus who made the acceptilatio is said to have a condictio on dissolution of the marriage. This is not a case of a third person receiving a thing destined to the claimant’s estate without contractual connection; the claim arises from the extraneus’ own acceptilatio/dotal arrangement with the husband. Nor is any traceable thing, substitute, or price identified in the defendant’s assets. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.

.7.393Ex qua certum petitur (numeratio)1AktionThe decisive scenario is the only explicit condictio: the extraneus who constituted a dowry by acceptilatio and, after dissolution of the marriage, has a condictio. The passage concerns recovery of the released dotal debt/obligation, including its term, condition, and surety; this is treated as a credit arising through formal words, not as an ownership claim or defective-credit case. The criterion is therefore satisfied.
