.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio is Gaius’s statement that in both stated cases the benefit acquired by the slave for the owner can be condicted from the owner. The claimant is not asserting subsisting ownership of a specific thing and seeking it as his property; he seeks restitution of an acquisition unjustly accruing to another through the slave’s stipulation. This is a personal enrichment-type claim, not a condictio standing in place of vindication. Conclusion: criterion not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last of the two cases: a slave serving in good faith stipulates in his own master’s name something that could have been acquired for the possessor, and the possessor seeks recovery from the master. This is an acquisition through stipulation and subsequent restitution claim, not a consensual mutuum of fungibles repayable in kind. No coinciding intention to create a loan for consumption appears. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.4.3100Persona alieni iuris (relationship entered into with a slave or son under paternal power)1AktionThe explicit condictio is Gaius’ statement that in both cases the thing can be condicted from the owner; it covers the two slave-stipulation cases together, with the second as the last factual instance. In each, the transaction is made through a slave, a persona alieni iuris, and the recovery is considered against the dominus who acquires through him, not against the slave personally. No iussum or praepositio making it the master’s own authorised transaction is indicated. Therefore the criterion is satisfied.
.4.4101Peculium increased and enriched (liability de peculio)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is Gaius’ statement that in both stated cases the thing acquired by the slave’s stipulation can be condicted from the owner. The passage concerns acquisition to the dominus proprietatis or true owner, not an enrichment of the slave’s peculium. No liability measured de peculio is asserted or discussed. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
