.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio is the last explicit one: where the giver transfers things for Seia before marriage on terms that they immediately become the recipient’s, and after notice says “condicam.” In that case ownership has passed to the recipient; the text contrasts this with the later alternative where the giver remains owner and must vindicate. The condictio is therefore a personal restitutionary claim, not a vindicatory claim based on subsisting ownership. Conclusion: not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the later one: the giver gives things on Seia’s behalf before marriage, with a variant where they become the recipient’s immediately and he says he will condicere after notice. This is a dowry-related transfer dependent on the marriage context, not a credit of fungibles to be returned in the same quantity and kind. No shared intention to make a mutuum appears. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.7.393Ex qua certum petitur (numeratio)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the later one: the giver gives things on Seia’s behalf before marriage, with ownership passing immediately to the recipient, and after notice says he will condicere. The claim is not based on the giver’s continued ownership, since the text expressly says the things become the recipient’s; vindicatio is reserved for the alternative where ownership did not pass. The condictio therefore concerns recovery of the given, determinate res after the dowry purpose fails. Conclusion: satisfied.
