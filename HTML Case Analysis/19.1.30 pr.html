.180Condictio ex mutuo0Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive passage is the last condictio scenario: the buyer unknowingly takes the stolen coins as peculium and consumes them, so a condictio lies against him. Although the text says the claimant’s property came to the defendant without cause, the coins have been consumed and cannot be reclaimed in specie. The claim is therefore a personal enrichment claim for value, not a vindicatory condictio based on subsisting ownership. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: the buyer, ignorant that the coins were stolen, removes them as if peculium and consumes them, so a condictio lies because the seller’s property came to him sine causa. This is not a consensual credit of fungibles to be repaid in kind; there is no giving as mutuum and no coinciding intention of lender and borrower. The criterion is therefore not satisfied.
