.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive passage is the final explicit condictio: a condictio against the heirs or other successors of one liable in a theft context. Although the text also mentions exhibition and vindication where the thing is possessed, this condictio follows the excluded actio furti and functions as the personal condictio furtiva/enrichment-type remedy, not as a vindication substitute based on subsisting ownership. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the final statement that a condictio lies against the heirs or other successors, despite their not being liable in the penal theft action. The passage concerns restitutionary liability connected with possession of a stolen thing, alongside actio ad exhibendum and vindicatio. It does not show any giving treated by both parties as a mutuum or any consensus to repay fungibles in kind. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
