.180Condictio ex mutuo0Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the donor’s recovery after a mortis causa gift: illo convalescente condictione teneor. The claim is a personal restitutionary liability arising from the conditional nature/failure of the mortis causa donation, not a vindicatory recovery of a specific thing as still owned by the claimant. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the donor’s recovery after an intended donatio mortis causa, where the recipient would be liable by condictio. The parties’ intentions concern a gift mortis causa and a further intended gift to the wife, not a credit of fungibles repayable by tantundem. There is no coinciding intention to create a mutuum. Conclusion: not satisfied.

The passage does not identify a fixed sum or specific thing; it refers only generally to “quod” was to be donated. The claim is tied to failure of the mortis causa basis, not to a stated certum credit. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
