.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio-passage is Julian’s statement that, where the familia commits theft, account must be taken not only of the double penalty but also of the condictio. This is a theft-based personal remedy included in calculating the claimant’s recovery, not a claim vindicating a specific thing as still owned by him. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio passage is Julian’s statement that, in the case of theft by a familia, account must be taken not only of the duplum but also of the condictio. This concerns liability arising from furtum and the calculation/imputation of remedies, not a consensual giving of money or fungibles as a mutuum. There is no coinciding intention of giver and recipient to create credit repayable in kind. Conclusion: not satisfied.

.7.393Ex qua certum petitur (numeratio)1AktionThe decisive passage is Julian’s statement that, besides the double penalty for theft, account must also be taken of the condictio in a case where the household slaves committed theft. In Roman law this refers to the condictio furtiva, by which the stolen thing or its value is pursued as a determinate object. Thus the condictio concerns a certum arising from theft. Conclusion: satisfied.
