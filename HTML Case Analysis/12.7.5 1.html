.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the dowry money given by a stepmother to a stepson or a daughter-in-law to a father-in-law where no valid marriage follows. The text grants condictio because there was no causa dotis dandae, i.e. a personal restitution claim for money paid without cause. It is not based on subsisting ownership of a specific thing or a vindicatory substitute. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the money given dotis nomine by a stepmother to a stepson or a daughter-in-law to a father-in-law, where the marriage did not occur and would be incestuous. The parties are treating the transfer as a dowry, not as a mutuum or credit repayable in the same quantity and kind. No coinciding intention to create a loan for consumption appears. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.7.393Ex qua certum petitur (numeratio)1AktionThe decisive scenario is the single dowry-payment case: a stepmother to stepson or daughter-in-law to father-in-law gives money as dos, no marriage follows, and the text says condictio competit. The object is pecunia given by payment, i.e. a determinate monetary enrichment recoverable because there was no causa dotis dandae. This satisfies the certum element.
