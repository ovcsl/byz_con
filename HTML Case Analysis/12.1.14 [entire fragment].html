.180Condictio ex mutuo0Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the creditor’s consumption of the coins paid by the filius familias contrary to the senatus consultum, where Marcellus says the condictio ceases. The passage contrasts vindication of existing coins with a denied condictio after consumption; the consumed coins could no longer be reclaimed in specie as the claimant’s property. Thus the condictio-passage does not show proceeding as owner by way of substitute vindication. Conclusion: not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the repayment by the filius familias of money borrowed contrary to the senatus consultum, with the creditor having consumed the coins. Although the antecedent transaction was a mutuum, the giving in the condictio passage is a solutio/repetition scenario, not a fresh credit intended by both parties to be repaid tantundem. Therefore the required consensus directed at a loan for consumption is not shown.

.4.2.270Datio (transfer of ownership)
.4.3100Persona alieni iuris (relationship entered into with a slave or son under paternal power)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the discussion whether a condictio lies after a filius familias, having borrowed contrary to the senatus consultum, paid coins that the creditor consumed. The relevant dependant is the son under paternal power, acting without any iussum or praepositio, and the matter is considered through the father’s position as vindicating or seeking repetition of the coins. Thus the alieni iuris criterion is satisfied.
.4.4101Peculium increased and enriched (liability de peculio)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the one where the son under paternal power pays money borrowed contrary to the senatus consultum and the creditor consumes the coins; Marcellus says the condictio ceases. The passage concerns vindicatio/repetition against the creditor, not enrichment of the son's peculium or a de peculio claim against the father. Liability measured by the peculium is neither asserted nor discussed. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
