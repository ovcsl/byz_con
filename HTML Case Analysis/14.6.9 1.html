.180Condictio ex mutuo0Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last case: money simply donated to the son is paid by him to his creditor, and Julian gives the father a condictio. Although the son lacked power to alienate the coins, the claim follows from the payment/restitution situation against the creditor, not from an in rem assertion to reclaim a specific subsisting thing as the father’s property. The criterion is therefore not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: money is simply donated to the son, he pays it to a creditor, and a condictio is said to belong to the father. This is framed as a donation followed by payment, not as a mutuum or credit repayable in tantundem between giver and recipient. There is therefore no coinciding intention to create a loan for consumption. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.4.3100Persona alieni iuris (relationship entered into with a slave or son under paternal power)1AktionThe decisive condictio is the final case: money simply donated to a filius familias is paid by him to a creditor, and Julian says the condictio belongs to the father. The claim is therefore considered on the father’s side because of the son’s act. Conclusion: satisfied.
.4.4101Peculium increased and enriched (liability de peculio)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the final one: money was simply donated to the son, he paid it to a creditor, and a condictio is said to belong to the father. The passage does not discuss enrichment of the son’s peculium or liability of the father measured by the peculium; the father is treated as claimant, not as defendant de peculio. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
