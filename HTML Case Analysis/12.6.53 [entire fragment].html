.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the later one: a third party, at the slave’s request, pays his own ten coins, which become the recipient’s, and the slave’s master is said to be able to claim by condictio. Because the text says the coins became the recipient’s, the claim is not based on subsisting ownership or recovery in specie. It is a personal restitutionary claim for money paid, so the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive passage is the last explicit condictio scenario: a third party, at the slave’s request, gives his own ten coins to satisfy the supposed testamentary condition. The giving is treated as a payment made in the slave’s name under an invalid testamentary premise, not as money advanced on credit to be repaid in kind. There is no coinciding intention of mutuum. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

The condictio is by the master on the slave’s account, but the specified constellation of a slave or son as the receiving/handling counterparty is not present. Conclusion: not satisfied.

.7.393Ex qua certum petitur (numeratio)1AktionThe decisive passage is the last distinct condictio scenario: a third person, at the slave’s request, paid his own ten coins, which became the recipient’s, and recovery by condictio is discussed. The object is a fixed sum, decem nummi, arising from a numeratio, not a claim based on continued ownership. Thus a certum is claimable by condictio.
