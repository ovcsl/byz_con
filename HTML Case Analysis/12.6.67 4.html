1D. 12.1 De rebus creditis si certum petetur et de condictione1Logik
.180Condictio ex mutuo0Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio-passage is the response that the heir cannot condicere the interest money paid by the minor during his lifetime. This is a claim to recover payments made as usurae, i.e. a personal restitutionary claim for a sum, not a vindicatory recovery of a specific thing as continuing owner. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the attempted recovery of the interest that the minor had paid on the money credited to him. The facts state that Lucius Titius 'pecuniam certam credidit' to Gaius Seius and that interest was paid on that principal, which shows both sides treated the transaction as a money loan for consumption. The later restitutio and minority do not negate that consensus. Conclusion: the criterion is satisfied.
.1.2.270Datio (transfer of ownership)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the heir’s attempted condictio for interest already paid by the deceased minor to the creditor. Although the background mentions a loan of principal, the transfer sought to be recovered is the payment of usurae in performance of that debt, not a datio credendi causa or quasi-mutuum. Thus the required mutuum-type passing of ownership is not shown for the decisive transfer. Conclusion: not satisfied.
