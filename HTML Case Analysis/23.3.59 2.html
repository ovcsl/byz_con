.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive passage is the last explicit condictio: “si mulieri donaturus dedisti, dominus condicet.” The master’s claim arises from the delivery made with donative intent toward the slave-woman and the legal attribution of that benefit to him, not from reclaiming an identifiable thing as his continuing property. It is a personal restitutionary condictio following a datio, not a vindicatory substitute. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: where the giver hands over the money intending to make a gift to the woman, and the owner condicet. That passage presents a donative causa, not a credit of fungibles to be repaid in kind, and no coinciding intention to create a mutuum appears. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.

Thus the dependant-side criterion is satisfied.
.4.4101Peculium increased and enriched (liability de peculio)0AktionThe decisive scenario is the last explicit condictio: where the giver intended a donation to the woman, the dominus condicet. The passage concerns the master’s recovery because the woman was under his power, not liability of the master or father measured by an enriched peculium. No peculium enrichment or de peculio limitation is shown. Conclusion: not satisfied.
