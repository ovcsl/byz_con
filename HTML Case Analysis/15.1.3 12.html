.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the explicit condici ex furtiva causa against a filius familias, with the related question whether a de peculio action lies against the pater or dominus to the extent of enrichment. This is a personal claim arising from theft/enrichment, not a reclaiming of a specific thing as still owned by the claimant. In Roman law the condictio furtiva is grounded in the delict, not in vindicatory ownership. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the explicit statement that a filius familias can be sued by condictio ex furtiva causa. This concerns enrichment from theft, not a consensual giving of fungibles as a mutuum. No coinciding intention to create credit repayable in kind appears. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.3.291Person Z is enriched at the expense of person Y, who should have had property from X. 1AktionThe decisive scenario is the single condictio ex furtiva causa arising from a theft by a filius familias or slave, considered together with the de peculio liability of the pater or dominus. The text expressly limits liability against the pater/dominus to the extent he became locupletior from the theft and to what came to him, showing identifiable enrichment in his assets without any contract with the claimant. This satisfies the required three-person enrichment constellation.

.4.3100Persona alieni iuris (relationship entered into with a slave or son under paternal power)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the express statement that a condictio ex furtiva causa lies against a filius familias, followed by the question whether an action de peculio is given against the father or master. The dependant is a son under paternal power, with the father/master addressed on the dependant’s account and only to the extent of peculium/enrichment from the theft. No iussum or praepositio appears; the theft points to unauthorised conduct. Conclusion: the criterion is satisfied.
.4.4101Peculium increased and enriched (liability de peculio)0AktionThe decisive scenario is the single furtive-cause condictio against a filius familias, with the connected question whether a de peculio action lies against the father or master. The text speaks of liability where the dominus is made richer by the theft or where something comes to the father, not of the peculium itself being increased by what was given. This is enrichment of the pater/dominus, not enrichment of the separate fund; the criterion is not satisfied.
