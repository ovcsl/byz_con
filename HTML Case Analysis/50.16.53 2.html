.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio-passage is the statement that, in theft committed by aid or counsel, one participant may be sued by condictio and another may not. This concerns condictio arising from furtum and the attribution of delictual participation, not a vindicatory claim to a specific thing as still owned by the claimant. Under Roman law the condictio furtiva is personal and delict-based even though the victim remains owner. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the single theft-related passage: depending on whether a person acted ope or consilio, condici potest against one and not against another. The text concerns liability arising from participation in furtum, not a consensual giving of fungibles as a mutuum. No coinciding intention of giver and recipient to create a credit repayable in kind appears. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.

.7.393Ex qua certum petitur (numeratio)1AktionThe decisive scenario is the single explicit condictio discussion: whether one who participates in theft by aid or counsel can be sued by condictio. Although the object is not named, the passage concerns furtum, and the theft-condictio pursues the stolen thing or its value as a certum. The text discusses the conditions for that condictio against participants, so the criterion is satisfied.
