.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the wife’s condicticia/condicatur against the husband after revoking a money donation used to buy existing assets. The text grounds the claim in enrichment from the donation and frames it as a personal recovery of money up to the value of the assets, not as vindication of a specific thing owned by the wife. It expressly contrasts this with the case where the wife’s thing remains and can be vindicated directly. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the wife’s condicticia claim after revoking money donated to her husband, where he used the donated money to buy existing assets. The text characterizes the transfer throughout as a donatio, not as a credit repayable in the same quantity and kind. There is no coinciding intention to create a mutuum. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.2.377Consumere (spend); Sine dolo malo (without fraud)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the wife’s donated money, with which the husband bought a movable or immovable thing, and the money is then condicted up to the value of the surviving thing. The passage presupposes that the donated pecunia has been spent in the purchase and no longer remains as identifiable coins in the husband’s hands; only the acquired res extant. Thus the consumption/spending element is satisfied.
