.180Condictio ex mutuo1Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the deposited ten coins which, after permission to use them, are treated as a mutuum; the later “poterit ei condici” concerns the same scenario. The claim lies quasi ex mutuo, with risk passing to the borrower, so the depositor proceeds as creditor for the loaned amount, not as owner reclaiming specific coins. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)1Logik
.1.2.168Consensus1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the single case of ten deposited coins which the depositor later permits the recipient to use, with condicere/condici discussed for that case. The text treats the deposit as becoming quasi mutua, and says the recipient who requested the mutuum begins to possess by intention. Thus both parties’ intentions coincide in treating the money as a loan for consumption repayable in kind. Conclusion: the criterion is satisfied.
.1.2.270Datio (transfer of ownership)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the deposited ten coins later permitted to be used, expressly treated as quasi-mutua and followed by condici. The recipient begins to possess animo, so the relationship becomes a quasi-mutuum and risk passes to him. This satisfies the required datio/ownership-treatment for fungibles in a quasi-mutuum. Conclusion: satisfied.
.1.389Mutuum claim at issue (the loan itself is the doctrine under discussion)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the deposit of ten later permitted to be used, treated as a quasi-mutuum; the later “poterit ei condici” belongs to the same scenario. The passage addresses whether the loan for consumption comes into existence before the coins are physically moved, because possession begins by intention and risk passes. This is a mutuum/quasi-mutuum formation question, so the criterion is satisfied.
