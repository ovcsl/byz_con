.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio is the final scenario: after an informal division without a judge, the true heir may have a condictio for the things ceded to the person he wrongly thought was coheir. The ground given is that there was no true settlement because of the mistake, so the claim is restitutionary from an invalid or causeless division. It is not framed as a vindicatory claim based on subsisting ownership of the specific things. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last one: the parties divided estate assets without a judge, and the true heir seeks condictio of things that passed to the supposed coheir. This is a partition/division made under mistaken coheir status, not a giving of fungibles as a mutuum to be repaid in kind. There is no coinciding intention to create credit. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.

The object is determinate: the very estate items allocated to that person in the division. The passage discusses their recovery by condictio, not merely an incertum claim. Therefore the criterion is satisfied.
