.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio is the last variant: after Titius has died, the promisor pays the ten to the stipulator and condicts the part of the slave from the stipulator rather than from Titius’ heir. The claim is framed as recovery of an indebitum arising from performance under the alternative promise, not as vindication of a thing that remains the claimant’s property. The condictio therefore functions as a personal restitutionary claim, not as an ownership-based substitute for rei vindicatio. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last variant: after Titius has died, the promisor pays ten to the claimant and seeks to condict the part of the slave from the claimant rather than from Titius’ heir. The text treats the numeratio as a solutio/indebitum in the context of an alternative promise, not as money credited to be repaid tantundem. No coinciding intention to create a mutuum appears. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.7.393Ex qua certum petitur (numeratio)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the combined case, including the final variant where after Titius’ death ten are paid and the promisor seeks by condictio the part of the slave previously delivered. The object claimed is a determinate share of a specific slave, treated as recoverable quasi indebitum after the alternative obligation has been satisfied by payment of ten. This is a certum pursued by condictio, so the criterion is satisfied.
