.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the case where a thing was given for inspection, the recipient says it is lost, but the claimant knows it is still with him: then he may sue for theft, condicere, or bring actio ad exhibendum. The condictio is linked to the wrongful/furtive retention, not framed as a vindication-like claim grounded simply in subsisting ownership. Under Roman reasoning this is a personal condictio arising from delictual conduct, not proceeding as owner of the specific thing. Conclusion: not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the case where a specific thing was given for inspection and is known still to be with the recipient, so the giver may sue for theft, condicere, or ad exhibendum. This is not a credit of fungibles to be consumed and repaid by tantundem, but a delivery for inspection with return of the same thing. There is no coinciding intention to create a mutuum. Conclusion: not satisfied.

.7.393Ex qua certum petitur (numeratio)1AktionThe decisive scenario is the single explicit condictio in which the thing given for inspection is known still to be with the recipient, so the giver may sue for theft, condicere, or bring ad exhibendum. The object is the specific inspected res, a determinate thing, and the context treats the condictio alongside the furtive remedy. Thus a certum is claimable by condictio.
