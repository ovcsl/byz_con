.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the woman’s delivery of dowry things before marriage on terms that they immediately become the man’s property, followed by failure of the marriage. The text says she must condicere eas, while contrasting this with vindicatio where ownership was to pass only after marriage. Thus the condictio is a personal restitution claim after ownership passed, not a claim as owner of the specific things. Conclusion: not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the woman’s giving of things as dowry before marriage on terms that they immediately become the man’s, followed by failure of the marriage, so that she must condicere them. The parties’ intention is directed to a dotal transfer, not to a mutuum or credit repayable in the same quantity and kind. Thus the required consensus for a loan for consumption is absent.

The text says she must condicere eas, i.e. reclaim those very things by condictio, not by vindication. The claim is therefore directed to determinate delivered objects whose ownership had passed to the man. Conclusion: satisfied.
