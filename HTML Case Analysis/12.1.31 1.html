.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe explicit condicere clauses concern the same acquisition-through-the-slave transaction; the last explicit form is Julian’s formulation of the seller’s condictio against the good-faith buyer. That claim is not put as a vindicatory assertion of subsisting ownership, but as a personal condictio arising from the delivery/transaction and the peculium adjustment. Since the thing was delivered to the defendant as acquirer and the remedy is not founded on ownership as such, the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive explicit condictio is the last one: Julian’s passage where the seller is said to be able to condicere against the bona fide buyer. That scenario concerns a sale/purchase context, not a giving of fungibles intended by both parties as a mutuum repayable in kind. No coinciding intention to create credit is shown. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.4.2.270Datio (transfer of ownership)
.4.3100Persona alieni iuris (relationship entered into with a slave or son under paternal power)1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the single transaction in which the slave, held by a bona fide possessor, used peculium belonging to his true master to buy another slave, with condictio discussed around that acquisition. The purchase was made by a servus alieni iuris without any iussum or praepositio being indicated, and the legal consequences are treated through the dominus: he may claim, and issues of de peculio liability arise. Thus the dependant’s act is legally referred to the master’s side. Conclusion: satisfied.
.4.4101Peculium increased and enriched (liability de peculio)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the last explicit one: Julian’s concern that the seller might condict against the bona fide purchaser. Although the surrounding passage discusses peculium and an actio de peculio for the price, the condictio itself is not based on the peculium being enriched by what was given; the purchased slave was delivered to the bona fide possessor, and the peculium money was spent. The liability discussed is connected with de peculio, but the required enrichment of the peculium is not shown. Conclusion: not satisfied.
