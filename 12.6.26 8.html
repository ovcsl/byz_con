.180Condictio ex mutuo0Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the final clause: one who knowingly pays after the peculium has been withdrawn has no condictio because he knowingly paid what was not due. This concerns recovery of an indebiti payment, not reclamation of a specific thing as subsisting owner. The claim, even though denied, is framed as a personal restitutionary condictio, not a vindicatory substitute. Therefore the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the knowing payment to a filius familias after his peculium had been withdrawn, where the payer has no condictio because he knowingly paid what was not due. The giving is treated as a payment of an indebitum, not as a credit of fungibles to be repaid in kind. There is no mutual intention to create a mutuum. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

.4.4101Peculium increased and enriched (liability de peculio)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the final case: the debtor knowingly pays the filius familias after the peculium has been withdrawn, and therefore has no condictio. The passage does not show that the payment enriched an existing peculium; rather, the peculium had been ademptum. Nor is the father’s liability discussed as de peculio or measured by the peculium. Conclusion: not satisfied.
