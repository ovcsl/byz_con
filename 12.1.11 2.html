1D. 12.1 De rebus creditis si certum petetur et de condictione1Logik
.180Condictio ex mutuo0Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the final one: the owner may condicere against the recipient if the fugitive slave’s coins have been consumed without fraud. The text contrasts this with vindicatio while the coins still exist, so the condictio is not reclaiming the specific coins as subsisting property. Because the thing has been consumed and the claim is personal restitutionary liability, the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus1AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the final one: the recipient consumed without dolus the coins that the fugitive slave had credited to him, and the master seeks condicere. The text frames the giving as credere/credendo, i.e. a money credit, and the recipient is treated as accipiens under that credit; the slave’s lack of authority affects acquisition of ownership, not the parties’ loan intention. Thus consensus toward a mutuum is shown.
.1.2.270Datio (transfer of ownership)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is the master’s condictio against the recipient after coins lent by a fugitive or unauthorized slave have been consumed without dolus. The text expressly contrasts an authorized peculium loan, which would be mutuum, with the fugitive/unauthorized slave’s crediting, which does not make the coins the recipient’s. Ownership therefore did not pass by datio because the slave lacked authority to alienate. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.

The criterion is not satisfied.
