1D. 12.1 De rebus creditis si certum petetur et de condictione1Logik
.180Condictio ex mutuo0Logik
.1.181Plaintiff is no owner1Logik
.1.1.112Plaintiff is owner0AktionThe decisive explicit condictio is the Papinian scenario: common coins are lent to the defendant and the claimant may condicere fifty. This is a personal claim for a determinate monetary amount arising from a loan, with the coins consumed, not a vindicatory recovery of specific property as owner. Conclusion: the criterion is not satisfied.
.1.25Contract mutuum (contract loan for consumption)0Logik
.1.2.168Consensus1AktionThe decisive explicit condictio scenario is Papinian’s case: I have credited/lent to you one hundred coins owned in common and can condicere fifty. The wording nummos credidero presents the transaction as a mutuum-type credit, and the common ownership of the coins affects ownership transfer, not the parties’ coinciding intention to lend and receive money for repayment in kind. Therefore the consensus element is satisfied.
.1.2.270Datio (transfer of ownership)0AktionThe decisive condictio scenario is Papinian’s supporting case: a co-owner lends common coins and seeks to condicere only his half. Because the coins are common, the lender cannot alienate the co-owner’s share, so ownership cannot pass in full by datio. The criterion is therefore not satisfied.

That consumption premise extends to the condictio analogy, with no contrary indication. Conclusion: satisfied.
